Formula Glossary

Every calculation this app makes, explained in one place. Rates and thresholds shown here are read live from the code -- if a rule ever changes, this page changes with it.

1. Per-Trade Formulas

Applied to each closed trade (a matched buy/sell or open/close pair) -- a direct port of the original spreadsheet's column logic.

Per-trade formulas: field name, formula, and explanation
Field Formula What it means
Realized Value quantity × sell_price Total proceeds from closing the position (fee-net -- see note below).
Cost Basis quantity × cost_price What the position originally cost (fee-inclusive).
Break Even strike_price + cost_price Per-unit, not multiplied by quantity. For Shares (no strike price), this collapses to just Cost Price. Informational only -- no other formula on this page depends on it.
Gain / Loss Realized Value − Cost Basis Dollar profit or loss on the closed trade.
% Gain / Loss Gain ÷ Cost Basis Return on the trade as a percentage. 0% if cost basis is 0.
Holding Period (months) (sell_date − buy_date).days ÷ 30 Approximate months held -- a simple day/30 conversion, not calendar-month-aware.
Gain / Month % Gain ÷ Holding Period (months) Return rate normalized by how long the trade was held. 0 if held under a month (rounds to 0 holding period).
Gain Type held > {{ long_term_threshold_days }} days ? "Long" : "Short" Determines which tax rate applies below.
Estimated Tax Gain × rate (gains only) Long-term rate {{ long_term_rate | pct }}, short-term rate {{ short_term_rate | pct }}. Losing trades owe $0 -- this column never nets a loss against a gain (see Section 5 for the monthly summary, which does net within a term).
Cumulative Investment running sum of Cost Basis Running total across all uploads ever made, in sell-date order -- never resets between statements.
Cumulative Gain running sum of Gain / Loss Same running-total treatment as Cumulative Investment.
Cumulative Gain % (Cum. Gain − Cum. Investment) ÷ Cum. Investment Overall return-to-date across the full trade history.

2. Tax Rules

Confirmed decisions -- flat rates, not bracket-based.

  • Long-term (held more than {{ long_term_threshold_days }} days): flat {{ long_term_rate | pct }}.
  • Short-term (held {{ long_term_threshold_days }} days or fewer): flat {{ short_term_rate | pct }}.
  • Tax only ever applies to gains at the per-trade level -- a loss never generates a negative tax / refund on its own row.

3. FIFO Lot Matching

How raw broker transactions become the closed trades that feed Section 1's formulas.

Current scope: options contracts only. Plain share Buy/Sell trades and expired-worthless contracts are intentionally excluded for now -- see below.
  • First-In-First-Out: the oldest open lot for a symbol is always closed first. No specific-lot override (a v2 feature if ever needed).
  • Options only: plain share trades are skipped entirely, regardless of action name.
  • Options multiplier: option contract quantities are converted to underlying-share-equivalent units by multiplying by {{ option_multiplier }} before matching (1 contract = {{ option_multiplier }} units).
  • Opening action: Buy to Open only.
  • Closing action: Sell to Close or Expired. An expired contract's Price/Fees/Amount are blank in the broker export -- the parser reads a blank money field as $0, so an expired contract closes automatically at $0 realized value (a total loss of the premium paid), matched against its opening lot exactly like a real sale. This is what keeps expired contracts from getting stuck showing up as still-open positions.
  • Cross-statement matching: matching runs across every upload ever made, not just one file -- a contract opened in one month's statement and closed in a much later one still matches correctly.
  • Partial fills: if a closing transaction is larger than the oldest lot, it consumes that lot fully and continues into the next-oldest lot -- can produce multiple closed-trade rows from one closing transaction.
  • Unmatched closes: if a close has no matching open lot in the uploaded history (usually because the position was opened before your earliest statement), it's flagged on the Review tab instead of silently guessed at.
  • Open positions: any lot with remaining unmatched units at the end of processing shows up as a currently-open position. Cost Value (Units × Open Price) shows how much money is actually still at stake in each one, with a grand total across all open positions at the bottom of the table.

4. Fees & Cost Basis

A deliberate, more tax-accurate departure from the original spreadsheet (which ignored fees).

  • Opening-side fees are folded into cost_price: fee_per_unit = fees ÷ units, added to the lot's unit price.
  • Closing-side fees are folded into sell_price: effective_sell_price = close_price − fee_per_unit.
  • Net effect: fees reduce your gain (or increase your loss) automatically, on both legs of the trade.

5. Monthly / Period Summaries

Roll-ups shown on the Dashboard and Overview tabs, built from the per-trade rows above.

Monthly and period summary formulas
Field Formula Notes
Realized Investment sum(Realized Value) Across all trades in the period.
Yield % sum(Gain) ÷ sum(Cost Basis) Blended return across the whole period, not an average of per-trade %s.
Win Rate count(Gain > 0) ÷ count(all trades) How often a trade was profitable, independent of how much it made or lost -- you can have a low win rate and still be net profitable if your wins run bigger than your losses (or vice versa), so read this alongside Avg Gain / Trade, not alone.
Avg Gain / Trade sum(Gain) ÷ count(all trades) Simple average across every closed trade in the period, wins and losses both included.
Avg Hold Period average(Sell Date − Buy Date, in days) Computed directly in days, not from the Trade Log's Hold Period Months column -- that column is itself a /30 approximation, so averaging days straight from the actual dates avoids compounding that rounding.
Top 5 Winners / Bottom 5 Losers the 5 highest and 5 lowest rows from the By Ticker breakdown Which specific tickers actually drove this period's total gain (or loss), biggest first in both directions. If you traded fewer than 10 distinct tickers in the period, the two lists never overlap -- a ticker already shown as a winner won't also appear in the losers list.
Month-by-Month / By Ticker (Monthly Performance tab) same Gain/Yield formulas above, grouped by calendar month or by ticker Scoped to this uploaded statement only by default -- pick "All periods (combined)" in the Viewing period dropdown for the same breakdowns across your entire trade history instead, without leaving the dashboard.
Month-over-Month / Year-over-Year (Overview charts) same Gain/Yield formulas above, grouped by calendar month or calendar year Both grouped by each trade's actual sell date, across your entire trade history (not just one upload) -- Year-over-Year is the exact same grouping logic as Month-over-Month, just rolled up one level coarser for spotting multi-year trends a month-by-month bar chart is too noisy to show.
Tax by Term (Short / Long) (gains + losses in that term) × rate Different from the per-trade Estimated Tax column -- this one nets losses against gains within the same term before applying the rate, so a losing trade can reduce the period's estimated tax. The per-trade column never does this.

6. CSV Parsing Rules

How raw broker export rows get cleaned up before anything else runs.

  • Option symbols like AMZN 08/21/2026 245.00 C are parsed into underlying ticker, expiration, strike, and right (Call/Put).
  • Adjusted contracts: a trailing digit right after the ticker with no space, e.g. AZN1 03/20/2026 90.00 C, marks an OCC-adjusted option (created when a corporate action -- merger, spinoff, special dividend, ADR ratio change, etc. -- forces the OCC to adjust existing contract terms). The digit is split off so the Ticker column always shows the real/unadjusted symbol (AZN, not AZN1) everywhere it appears (Trade Log and Needs Review both split Ticker / Expiration / Strike / Type into their own columns now, instead of one raw symbol string) -- a small i icon next to the ticker flags which rows these are (hover it for the explanation). This does not change the 100x options multiplier -- verified against a real adjusted trade where the standard multiplier reproduced the broker's reported dollar amount exactly.
  • Settlement vs. effective date: rows formatted as 08/24/2026 as of 08/21/2026 (common on Expired rows) use the as-of date -- what actually matters for holding period and tax-year purposes -- not the later settlement date.
  • Account label: pulled from the uploaded filename via a masked pattern like XXX939 (e.g. Joint_Tenant_XXX939_Transactions_...csv).

7. Income Tab (Bonus, Not Tax-Relevant)

Shown for context only -- these never feed into the gain/loss or tax formulas above. Dividends and fees only -- transfers moved to their own Transfers tab, see Section 7a below.

  • Included actions: Cash Dividend, Qualified Dividend, ADR Mgmt Fee, Foreign Tax Paid.
  • Totals are a simple sum of the Amount column per action type, plus a grand total across all of them.

7a. Transfers Tab (Cash Movements, Not Income)

Money moving in or out of this account -- not something you earned, so kept separate from the Income tab. Also shown for context only; never feeds into gain/loss or tax.

  • Included actions: MoneyLink Transfer, Journal.
  • Relabeled for clarity: the broker's raw action names for both of these aren't obvious, so the tab shows a friendlier label instead (sign-aware, since either can in principle run either direction): MoneyLink Transfer -- Schwab's linked-bank-account transfer -- shows as Withdrawal (money left to your bank) or Deposit (money arrived from your bank). Journal -- an internal transfer to/from a different brokerage account (e.g. a family member's), not a bank -- shows as Journal Transfer (to Another Account) or Journal Transfer (from Another Account).
  • Totals and card filters work exactly like the Income tab's -- click a card to filter the table, click it again (or "Total") to clear.

8. Recommended By / Reason / Notes (Your Own Data, Not the Broker's)

Three free-text columns at the right edge of the Trade Log that you fill in yourself -- the broker CSV never contains this, it's purely for your own tracking.

  • Auto-save: each field saves the moment you click away from it (a quick green border flash confirms it saved; red means it failed) -- no submit button, and it won't reload the page or lose your current filters.
  • Survives re-uploads: stored in a separate table keyed by trade identity (account/ticker/strike/expiration/buy date/sell date/quantity), not by row ID -- because the Trade Log itself gets fully rebuilt from scratch every time you upload or delete a statement. Your notes stick around across that rebuild.
  • Gain by Recommender (Overview page): groups every closed trade by whatever name you typed into Recommended By, showing their total gain, yield, and trade count as a collapsed summary row -- click a name to drill down and see the individual trades (ticker, sell date, qty, gain/loss, reason, notes) behind that total. Trades with nothing filled in land under Unspecified rather than being dropped, so the group totals always add up to the grand total.

9. Multiple Accounts

Every statement's account is pulled from the broker's masked account number in the filename (e.g. XXX939) and attached to every trade in that statement.

  • Segregated matching: opening and closing legs are only ever matched against each other within the SAME account, even if two accounts happen to trade the identical contract at the same time -- a Buy in one account can never get paired with a Sell in a different one.
  • Account filter: once you've uploaded statements from more than one account, an Account dropdown appears at the top of the dashboard next to Viewing period. It's a real page reload, not a client-side row-hider -- picking an account re-scopes every tab (Trade Log, Monthly Performance, Gains/Losses/Tax, Income, Transfers, Needs Review) down to just that account's data, including all the totals and charts, not just which rows are visible. "All accounts" shows the combined book, same as before this existed. With only one account in the system, the dropdown stays hidden to avoid clutter.
  • Viewing period and Account always stay in sync, both directions: a statement belongs to exactly one account. Picking Account=XXX111 jumps Viewing period to XXX111's own most recent statement if you were looking at a different account's, and greys out (disables) every other account's individual periods in that dropdown. With Account: All accounts, EVERY account's individual periods are disabled -- only "All periods (combined)" is pickable, until you choose a specific account. Going the other way: landing on one specific statement (via a link, a fresh upload, or typing a URL) with no account filter set auto-selects that statement's own account instead of leaving "All accounts" showing next to what is actually just one account's data. This exists because Monthly Performance/Income/Transfers are scoped to "this specific statement," so any mismatch between the two dropdowns used to render those three tabs empty or misleadingly narrow.
  • Overview has the same filter: the Overview page gets its own independent Account dropdown (top-right, next to "Performance Overview") so you can see how you're performing on one account in isolation -- Realized Investment, Total Gain, Cumulative/Month-over-Month/Year-over-Year charts, and Gain by Recommender all re-derive from just that account's trades. Defaults to all accounts combined when you first land on the page. The Uploaded Statements table at the bottom always lists every statement regardless of this filter, same as the dashboard's Viewing period dropdown.
  • "All periods (combined)": the top option in the dashboard's Viewing period dropdown. Trade Log and Needs Review always show your full history no matter what's selected there, but Monthly Performance / Gains-Losses-Tax / Income / Transfers normally only cover ONE statement -- which made "Account: All accounts" confusingly still show just one narrow date range on those four tabs. Picking "All periods (combined)" makes those four tabs combine every statement too, still respecting whatever Account filter is active (so "All periods" + "Account: XXX111" means XXX111's entire history, not everyone's). There's no single statement to delete in this view, so "Delete this statement" hides itself.

10. Duplicate Transaction Detection

Some brokers re-export your ENTIRE history every time (a "Jan 1 to today" download), not just the new period. Re-uploading that kind of file later in the year would otherwise re-insert everything already in the system and double-count every total.

  • Automatic: every row in a new upload is checked against every transaction already saved (matched on account, date, action, symbol, description, quantity, price, fees, and amount together) -- exact matches are silently skipped, only genuinely new rows get added.
  • Feedback either way: if some rows were new, the dashboard banner tells you how many were added and how many were skipped as duplicates. If the ENTIRE file was already in the system, no new statement is created at all, and the upload page explains why.
  • Still recommended: if you know an old statement is being fully superseded (not just extended), deleting it before uploading the replacement keeps your Uploaded Statements list tidy -- but forgetting to won't corrupt your numbers anymore.
